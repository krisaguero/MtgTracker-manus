What if your next deck only needed one clear job?

Our new Commander feature explores eight commanders from The Commander’s Quarters video **“Commanders With Super Simple Builds That Will DOMINATE Your Playgroup!”** The common thread is focus: each commander gives the deck a visible pressure point and makes it easier to decide what belongs in the list.

### Eight commanders, eight clean plans

**Jasmine Boreal of the Seven** turns vanilla creatures into difficult-to-block threats. The deckbuilding challenge is to grow the board without accidentally giving those creatures abilities that undermine the plan.

**Yargle and Multani** starts close to the 21 commander-damage threshold. Add evasion, protection, and a few ways to push one huge attack through.

**Arcades, the Strategist** transforms defenders into card-drawing attackers. Cheap walls, high toughness, toughness pumps, and blink effects form the core loop.

**Edric, Spymaster of Trest** rewards cheap evasive creatures with cards. Deploy a low-to-the-ground board, connect in combat, and turn small attackers into a full hand.

**Ovika, Enigma Goliath** turns noncreature spells into hasty Phyrexian Goblins. Mana rocks, card draw, and pump effects all become part of the same token engine.

**Omnath, Locus of Mana** banks green mana and converts patience into power. The plan is simple: produce mana, preserve it, multiply it, and choose the right moment to strike.

**Mr. Orfeo, the Boulder** doubles a creature’s power and then converts that power into combat or sacrifice damage. It is a clean home for oversized creatures and “Fling” effects.

**Ruric Thar, the Unbowed** makes noncreature spells painful while encouraging a creature-heavy build. The restriction is part of the deck’s identity, not an afterthought.

**[Read: Simple builds, serious pressure: eight commanders that can dominate a playgroup](https://mtg-tracker-manus-current.vercel.app/blog/simple-builds-that-dominate-your-playgroup)**

After reading, open the [Commander Library](https://mtg-tracker-manus-current.vercel.app/commander), choose one commander, and write down three jobs for the first draft:

1. What should the deck do in its first few turns?
2. What happens when the commander is removed?
3. How does the deck actually close a game?

Then test the list against your real playgroup. A focused plan still needs interaction, recovery, a reasonable curve, and a table that enjoys the experience.
