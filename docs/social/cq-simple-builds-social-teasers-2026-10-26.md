## Reddit — suggested post

I wrote a companion feature for The Commander’s Quarters video [“Commanders With Super Simple Builds That Will DOMINATE Your Playgroup!”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x8KK0-U8FAc).

The central idea is that a simple deck can still be powerful when its plan is focused. Instead of treating “simple” as a synonym for weak, the article asks whether each commander gives the deck a clear, repeatable job:

- **Jasmine Boreal of the Seven:** build around vanilla creatures and preserve their near-unblockable angle.
- **Yargle and Multani:** use a huge commander, evasion, and protection to reach 21 commander damage.
- **Arcades, the Strategist:** play cheap high-toughness defenders that draw cards and attack with toughness.
- **Edric, Spymaster of Trest:** deploy evasive creatures and turn combat damage into a steady stream of cards.
- **Ovika, Enigma Goliath:** cast mana rocks, draw spells, and other noncreature spells to make hasty Phyrexian Goblins.
- **Omnath, Locus of Mana:** store green mana, multiply it, and turn the resource bank into a Voltron-style threat.
- **Mr. Orfeo, the Boulder:** double a creature’s power and convert that power into combat or sacrifice-to-damage finishes.
- **Ruric Thar, the Unbowed:** lean into creatures while making noncreature spells painful for the table.

The feature includes short, timestamped excerpts from the public captions and links back to the original video. It also separates the exact video from another similarly titled article that covers a different commander list.

The main takeaway: a focused plan still needs interaction, recovery, a reasonable curve, and an honest test against the people you actually play with. “Dominate” is the video’s framing—not a guaranteed win-rate claim.
