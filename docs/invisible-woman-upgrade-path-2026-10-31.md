# Invisible Woman Upgrade Path — The Fantastic Four

**Commander:** Invisible Woman, Sue Storm
**Deck:** The Fantastic Four — Marvel Super Heroes Commander precon
**Build goal:** A defensive four-color Wall/token deck that converts noncreature spells into a growing board, protects that board, and wins through a safe, amplified combat step.

## 1. What this commander actually wants to do

Invisible Woman should not be upgraded as a generic four-color pile. Her best version has three connected jobs:

1. **Cast a noncreature spell before combat.** This turns on the Fantastic Four’s beginning-of-combat payoff and gives the deck its signature Wall/token production.
2. **Build a board that is difficult to attack through.** The 0/3 Walls buy time, protect planeswalkers and life total, and create the material needed for token payoffs.
3. **Convert defense into lethal pressure.** High-toughness payoffs, token doublers, anthem effects, evasion, and attack-trigger support turn an initially passive board into a decisive combat step.

The most important sequencing rule is simple: **do not spend your only noncreature spell too early if it is safe to wait.** In many games the correct line is to develop a mana source or creature on the previous turn, then cast a cheap instant or sorcery in the main phase before combat so Invisible Woman’s trigger is active.

The deck should therefore value cheap, flexible noncreature spells more highly than expensive creatures that merely have large stats.

## 2. Recommended final architecture

A tuned Invisible Woman build should aim roughly for:

| Function | Target | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Lands | 37–38 | Four colors and multiple activated abilities demand reliable mana. |
| Ramp/fixing | 10–12 | Preserve the stock two-mana ramp while adding a few creature-based or token-based mana engines. |
| Cheap noncreature spells | 14–18 | Keeps the commander online before combat and gives the deck interaction on opposing turns. |
| Token/Wall payoffs | 10–12 | Makes each Invisible Woman trigger matter beyond a single 0/3 body. |
| Protection | 7–9 | The board is the engine; protect it from sweepers and commander removal. |
| Card advantage | 8–10 | Prevents the deck from running out of spells after establishing a board. |
| Finishers | 4–6 | Gives the deck a clean way to end games instead of producing harmless tokens indefinitely. |

The stock precon already supplies useful ramp—**Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Farseek, Cultivate, Three Visits,** and **Chromatic Lantern**—so the first upgrade dollars should go toward protection, token scaling, and spell payoffs rather than more mana rocks.

## 3. The staged upgrade plan

### Stage One — make the commander reliable

Start with cards that improve the deck every time it casts a spell and that remain useful when the battlefield is empty.

**Add first:**

- **Swords to Plowshares** — efficient creature interaction that leaves mana available for development.
- **Heroic Intervention** — protects the Wall/token board and important enchantments from sweepers.
- **Counterspell** — protects Invisible Woman or a high-value engine at a very low cost.
- **Beast Within** — answers any permanent type and is rarely dead.
- **Nature’s Lore** — adds another early land-ramp spell and fixes colors.
- **An Offer You Can’t Refuse** — a cheap instant that can turn on the commander while leaving mana open.
- **Swiftfoot Boots** — protects Invisible Woman and gives haste when an attack trigger matters immediately.
- **Propaganda** — buys the turns needed to build a defensive board.
- **Brainstorm** — a low-cost instant-speed trigger that improves card quality when paired with shuffle lands.
- **Restorative Technique** — inexpensive recursion for a destroyed token engine, artifact, or enchantment.

These ten cards solve the first problem: the deck should reliably have a spell to cast before combat without sacrificing all of its mana or exposing the commander.

**First-stage play pattern:**

- Turns 1–2: develop a land or ramp piece.
- Turn 3: cast Invisible Woman only when you can reasonably protect her or gain immediate value.
- Before the next combat: cast a cheap instant or sorcery, ideally one that also answers a threat or protects the board.
- Use the Walls to absorb attacks while saving premium removal for permanents that can invalidate the token plan.

### Stage Two — turn every spell into a wider board

Once the commander is reliable, add engines that multiply the value of each noncreature spell.

**Core token engines:**

- **Shark Typhoon** — the best all-around addition. Its cycling mode is a cheap instant-speed trigger, while the battlefield mode turns future noncreature spells into flying threats.
- **Monastery Mentor** — produces Monk tokens from the deck’s normal spell pattern and can become a real combat threat.
- **Third Path Iconoclast** — a budget-friendly way to make bodies from instants and sorceries.
- **Young Pyromancer** — another inexpensive token engine for a spell-dense build.
- **Caretaker’s Talent** — rewards token creation with card advantage and helps the deck keep chaining spells.
- **Impact Tremors** — converts every new token into damage, preventing the deck from stalling behind a large but harmless board.

At this stage, Invisible Woman is no longer creating one defensive Wall at a time. A single pre-combat spell can produce the commander’s Wall, a Monk or Elemental, and additional value from token-trigger effects.

### Stage Three — make the Wall board lethal

Do not buy all of these cards automatically. Choose this package if you want Invisible Woman—not Mister Fantastic—to remain the permanent commander.

**Wall and toughness package:**

- **High Alert** — turns the high-toughness board into an attacking force and supports the deck’s defensive posture.
- **Assault Formation** — a second high-toughness payoff; strongest when the creature base is deliberately Wall-heavy.
- **Axebane Guardian** — turns a cluster of Walls into a serious mana engine.
- **Arcades, the Strategist** — a strong alternate commander and a powerful 99-card payoff for a dedicated Wall build.

**Token multiplication package:**

- **Parallel Lives** — doubles Wall, Shark, Monk, and other token production.
- **Adrix and Nev, Twincasters** — another token doubler that also contributes a body.
- **Cryptolith Rite** — converts a wide token board into colored mana for activated abilities and a large finishing turn.

**Recommended order:** **Caretaker’s Talent → High Alert → Impact Tremors → Cryptolith Rite → Parallel Lives.** Buy **Adrix and Nev** or **Arcades** after testing whether the deck is actually producing enough creatures to justify them. Parallel Lives is powerful but is not the first purchase because it is expensive and does nothing without an active token engine.

### Stage Four — protect the engine and close games

A token deck loses when it spends several turns building a board and then loses everything to one sweeper. Protection should be layered rather than concentrated in one premium card.

**Protection priorities:**

- **Heroic Intervention** — first purchase.
- **Swiftfoot Boots** — protects the commander without preventing her from being targeted by your own beneficial effects.
- **Counterspell** and **An Offer You Can’t Refuse** — efficient stack interaction.
- **Clever Concealment** — already in the stock list and especially valuable with a wide board because of convoke.
- **Invisible Force Field** — already in the stock list; preserve it unless the final build is moving away from a defensive plan.
- **Fierce Guardianship** and **Deflecting Swat** — premium upgrades only after the lower-cost protection package is complete.

**Ways to finish:**

- A wide board amplified by **High Alert** or **Assault Formation**.
- Repeated token entry damage from **Impact Tremors**.
- A large **Shark Typhoon** turn followed by evasion and protection.
- A token-doubled board with **Cryptolith Rite**, allowing the deck to cast multiple spells and pay for activated abilities in one turn.
- Invisible Woman’s attack trigger creating a safe, unblockable attack for the most important creature when the board is ready to close.

The deck should have at least two different finishing routes. Do not rely only on combat; a board stall is common in Commander, and Impact Tremors or a similar payoff gives the deck a way to make progress without exposing every creature.

## 5. Suggested stock-card cuts

The exact cuts should follow several playtests, but these are the first places I would look in the current list because they are less central to an Invisible Woman token/spell plan:

| Cut candidate | Why it is vulnerable |
|---|---|
| Galactus, Devourer of Worlds | Huge threat but does not reward noncreature spells or token production; better in a dedicated ramp build. |
| Silver Surfer, Galactus’s Herald | The least connected to the Wall/token engine in the stock list. |
| Genesis Ultimatum | Powerful but expensive; often competes with the turn where the deck wants to cast several cheaper spells. |
| Mind’s Dilation | High ceiling but slow and not a reliable pre-combat trigger. |
| Annie Joins Up | Stronger in a dedicated legendary-trigger build than in a token-focused Invisible Woman build. |
| Mirage Mirror | Flexible, but mana-intensive and less important once the deck has focused engines. |
| Path of Discovery | Useful in creature-heavy explore shells, but not essential to a spell-and-Wall plan. |
| Taunt from the Rampart | Defensive effect can be redundant once Propaganda and a Wall board are online. |
| Seize the Day | Keep only if the deck routinely has a lethal attack; otherwise it is a narrow extra-combat effect. |
| Tragic Arrogance | Strong reset, but consider whether it repeatedly destroys your own token advantage. |

The cleanest first nine-for-nine swap is the same general direction used by independent upgrade analysis: remove slower or off-plan cards such as **Galactus, Silver Surfer, First Family, Tragic Arrogance, Collective Effort, Path of Discovery, Bovine Intervention,** and other cards that do not directly advance the token/spell engine, then add a compact package of spell payoffs and protection.

Do not cut all sweepers. Invisible Woman is a board-building commander, so at least two flexible resets should remain for games where another deck develops a board faster than you do.

## 6. Budget versions

### About $25

Buy **Swords to Plowshares, Counterspell, Beast Within, Nature’s Lore, An Offer You Can’t Refuse, Swiftfoot Boots, Propaganda, Third Path Iconoclast, Young Pyromancer,** and **Impact Tremors**. This version improves consistency, interaction, and the commander’s ability to convert spells into a board without relying on expensive staples.

### About $50

Add **Shark Typhoon, Monastery Mentor, Storm-Kiln Artist, Archmage Emeritus, Caretaker’s Talent,** and **Lithoform Engine**. This is the best value breakpoint: the deck starts to feel like a deliberate Invisible Woman build rather than a stock four-color precon.

### About $100+

Add **Parallel Lives, Adrix and Nev, Twincasters, Cryptolith Rite, Improvisation Capstone, Echocasting Symposium,** and **Germination Practicum**. Add premium protection only if the deck is already performing well and you expect those cards to move into other decks.

## 7. Mulligans and play patterns

A good opening hand usually contains three lands or two lands plus a reliable ramp spell, one early noncreature spell, and either a token engine, protection spell, or card-advantage piece. Hands with several expensive creatures but no cheap spell should generally be shipped back.

Prioritize fixing **blue and green early** when possible. Blue provides the cheap interaction and card flow; green supports the ramp and the Wall/toughness mana engine. White and red become more important when you are preparing to activate Fantastic Four attack abilities or cast removal, but the deck should avoid keeping a hand that can only produce one color.

Do not cast the commander automatically on turn four. If the table is not pressuring you, it is often better to cast a value engine first or hold up protection. Invisible Woman is more valuable after you can immediately follow her with a spell and a combat trigger than she is as an unprotected four-mana creature.

Use token doublers after an engine is established whenever possible. Casting **Parallel Lives** with no token source is a low-impact turn; casting it before a turn where Invisible Woman, Shark Typhoon, and Monastery Mentor can all trigger creates a much larger swing.

## 8. Final recommendation

Build Invisible Woman as **defensive spellslinger tokens**, not as pure Walls and not as generic four-color value. The first version should make a Wall every turn, create additional bodies from cheap noncreature spells, protect those bodies, and win with a single well-timed combat step.

The most important purchases are **Shark Typhoon, Monastery Mentor, Third Path Iconoclast, Caretaker’s Talent, Heroic Intervention, Swiftfoot Boots, Propaganda,** and a small package of cheap interaction. Only after that engine is working should the deck buy **Parallel Lives, Adrix and Nev, Cryptolith Rite,** or premium free counterspells.
