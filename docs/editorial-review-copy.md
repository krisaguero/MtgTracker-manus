# Shuffle up from anywhere: remote games and first-hand tests for Brawlternatives

October 18, 2026 · Draft for review

Local route: `/blog/remote-play-shuffle-tests`

Choose a webcam table, a browser table, or a digital client—and take the right decklist with you.

A new brew deserves a few opening hands before it deserves a shopping cart. Our Brawlternative shelf is built for that curious stage: smaller, theme-first decks that let an idea get onto the table without pretending it is already a polished Commander list. You can test that idea alone, or invite a friend who lives nowhere near your kitchen table. The important part is choosing the right kind of table.

## Convoke: keep the paper-table feeling

[Convoke](https://convoke.games/en/) is a webcam Magic platform. Its official site describes friend invites, public tables, card recognition, and in-game card search. This is the natural starting point if you and your friends want to play with physical cards while seeing each other's boards. Set up a camera, agree on the house format, and make sure both players can read the battlefield before starting.

Convoke explicitly documents **Moxfield decklist import**. That is not a promise that this site's Copy button can load a Convoke table directly. Copy or download our generic list, create or update your deck in Moxfield using its own import controls, then follow Convoke's Moxfield import flow. Review the imported names and quantities rather than assuming the handoff did everything.

## Aura0: a browser table, not an assumed import shortcut

[Aura0](https://aura0.app/) presents an online table with deck selection, drawing, discard and exile zones, an action log, and room-link controls. It offers another way to explore remote tabletop play with friends. The public table is enough to understand the idea; it does not establish a documented direct-import contract for our deck exports.

Open the homepage and inspect the deck controls yourself before planning a session. Use a room you create or are invited to use. We are not publishing a private room link, joining anyone's existing game, or claiming that a deck can be pushed into Aura0 with one click.

## Arena and MTGO: copy once, then import in the client

Our export controls are a one-click **copy or download**, not a one-click installation inside another app. [Arena's official instructions](https://mtgarena-support.wizards.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049857771-Importing-a-Deck) say to copy the list, open **Decks → Import**, and inspect the result. Choose the Arena output so the commander, deck, and any sideboard remain separate. Card availability and the selected format still decide whether the result can actually be played.

[Magic Online's deckbuilding guide](https://www.mtgo.com/getting-started/getting-started-deckbuilding) supports importing text files, pasted lists, and its own .dek files. Our MTGO option provides text; it does not invent the catalog IDs needed for a genuine .dek file. Review zones in the client, especially the commander. MTGO's [official forum discussion](https://forums.mtgo.com/index.php?threads/text-file-commander-decks-import-in-backwards-zones.3196/) explains why commander position in text imports matters.

None of these exports turns a custom 60-card list into an officially supported format. These Brawlternatives are **custom Commander previews, not official Brawl and not regulation 100-card Commander**. Some cards or whole builds may be unavailable or unplayable in either digital client. Agree on a compatible house-rule table instead of treating a successful text import as a legality certificate.

## A useful ten-hand test

Before meeting a friend, deal a handful of opening hands with your physical list or your chosen deck tester. Note the lands, the first meaningful play, and whether the deck can do its signature thing. Shuffle fully between attempts; do not keep rearranging a bad hand until it looks good. Ten hands are a quick diagnostic, not proof that a deck is balanced.

For Mothman, watch whether the counter plan develops without running out of cards. For Rakdos, check whether you can make an opponent lose life **before** trying to cast the commander. For Karlov, count separate life-gain events rather than just the amount of life gained. Then play real games: interaction changes everything.

Ready to pick a list? [Browse the whole Brawlternative shelf](/blog/brawlternative-shelf-roundup), or try one of the [nine Battle Box decks](/blog/battle-box-october-17-recap). If you prefer real cards, use **Buy Online** on the deck page; proxy-print guidance stays optional and secondary. No export button buys cards or spends wildcards for you.

# Seven Brawlternatives, three finished proposals, and a reason to shuffle each one

October 18, 2026 · Draft for review

Local route: `/blog/brawlternative-shelf-roundup`

The full shelf in one place, with Mothman, Rakdos, and Karlov source lists preserved beside their proposed finishes.

The appeal of this shelf is not that every deck does the same efficient thing. It is that each one asks a different question. Can artifacts turn energy into staying power? Can the graveyard become a second hand? Can a tiny life-gain trigger become a serious threat? These seven builds are invitations to find out.

They share a house label: **Brawlternatives—custom 60-card Commander-preview decks**. They are not official Brawl decks or regulation 100-card Commander decks, and there is no claim that they are tournament-ready or evenly matched. Choose opponents and house rules accordingly.

## The three proposed finishes

### Mothman: let the counters tell the story

[The Wise Mothman](/brawl-box/the-wise-mothman) keeps its Fallout-forward radiation, mill, and +1/+1-counter identity. The preserved submission totals 59 cards. The proposed revision corrects three names to **Harold and Bob, First Numens**, **Strong, the Brutish Thespian**, and **Piper Wright, Publick Reporter**. These are name repairs, not three new deck slots.

The actual slot changes are **Abzan Falconer out; Fathom Mage and Inspiring Call in**. Falconer is outside the commander's Sultai color identity. Fathom Mage turns counters placed on itself into cards, while Inspiring Call draws for creatures you control with +1/+1 counters and gives those creatures indestructible for the turn. Together they finish the count at **60**, retain the **22-land** base, and support the deck's existing story rather than replacing it with expensive staples. This is a budget-minded direction, not a live price quote.

### Rakdos: the finish is understanding the sequence

[Rakdos, Lord of Riots](/brawl-box/rakdos-lord-of-riots) already has **60 cards and 21 lands**. Its proposed completion keeps every slot, including Leechridden Swamp. The important repair is the explanation: an opponent must already have lost life that turn before you cast Rakdos. Once Rakdos is on the battlefield, his reduction affects later creature spells and reduces **generic**, not colored, mana. It does not refund the spell you already cast.

Leechridden Swamp is a setup option, not a free shortcut. It enters tapped; its life-loss activation costs black mana and a tap, and requires two black permanents. Test the early turns honestly before promising spectacular discounted threats. The deck's theatrical payoff is the point, but its mana and sequencing still need to cooperate.

### Karlov: keep both Daxos cards, keep their jobs distinct

[Karlov of the Ghost Council](/brawl-box/karlov-of-the-ghost-council) finishes at **60 cards** with the approved **Crested Sunmare** correction and **Daxos the Returned** replacing Licia. **Daxos, Blessed by the Sun stays**; the two Daxos cards are different cards with different roles. The proposal keeps the source's 20-land base rather than claiming it has been optimized.

Small life-gain events grow Karlov, while the enchantment thread gives Daxos the Returned something to build around. Karlov's exile ability still asks for white-black mana and six counters. A large commander does not gain evasion just because its counters came easily. The splashy finishers are reasons to test the deck, not evidence that it has already passed the test.

Each of these pages separates the proposed list and reasoning from the preserved source history. You can inspect and export the intended version without losing the submitted one. [Scryfall](https://scryfall.com/) card links on the pages supply the rules text behind the changes; representative art is not a statement about which printing anyone owns.

## Four more personalities already on the shelf

[Dr. Madison Li](/brawl-box/dr-madison-li) brings energy-fueled artifacts, enter-the-battlefield value, and recursion. Start here if you enjoy finding a useful interaction among ordinary-looking pieces.

[Araumi of the Dead Tide](/brawl-box/araumi-of-the-dead-tide) explores self-mill and encore. Remember that encore depends on the number of opponents; a duel does not produce the same board as a multiplayer pod.

[Marchesa, the Black Rose](/brawl-box/marchesa-the-black-rose) leans into Grixis treason: borrowed creatures, sacrifice, fiery threats, and recursion. Check whose creatures you control and when the relevant triggers happen rather than treating every theft effect as permanent ownership.

[Taigam, Sidisi's Hand](/brawl-box/taigam-sidisis-hand) offers blue-black draw-go control, upkeep selection, removal, and graveyard-fueled creature shrinking. It is the patient choice when you would rather hold an answer than race.

Pick a personality, read its plan, and give it a few opening hands. Our [remote-play guide](/blog/remote-play-shuffle-tests) explains which services support a webcam table, a browser table, or a documented text import. On each deck page, **Buy Online** is the main physical-card route; optional proxy help is secondary. Neither route infers what is missing from anyone's collection.

## Choose the game you want to play

**Goblin Cavalcade** and **Lightning Burn** ask immediate questions. Goblins turn a crowd of small attackers into pressure; Burn trades flexibility for a much shorter clock. They are good starting points when you want to find out whether another brew can survive an early challenge.

**Kavu Beastmaster** and **RG Eldrazi Ramp & Stompy** move toward larger threats. Kavu mixes early exchanges with oversized combat bodies; Eldrazi spends early resources on getting to its finishers. Watch the turns before the big spell, not just how impressive the big spell looks.

**Oldschool Zombie Tribal** makes attrition matter through creatures, recursion, and repeated exchanges. **Azorius Control** approaches the same long game from another angle: answers first, a protected finisher later. Neither name guarantees a balanced matchup against the fastest lists.

**Theros Boros Soldiers** rewards building a board and targeting the right creature. **Simic Evasive Tempo & Midrange** mixes bounce, evasive threats, and incremental value. **Mono-White Lifegain & Sunbond Heroic** connects small life-gain starts with growing threats. These are distinct play patterns, not a power ranking.

**Buy Online** is the primary shopping action on the deck pages. Use the shopping list and retailer search to choose editions, condition, quantities, and shipping yourself. There is no automatic retailer checkout, no promise of stock or a live deck price, and no purchase is made by following an article link.

Proxy-print help is a smaller, optional route for groups that allow it. Source annotations and rules warnings remain visible; they are not evidence that a visitor owns or lacks any card. Incomplete scans cannot establish a shopping gap.

## Before the first game

Read the deck's source warnings and any house-rule overlay. A deck having 60 cards does not certify normal constructed legality: copy-limit exceptions or other source issues may still need agreement. An optional boss or secret objective is a house rule, not printed card text.

For a remote evening, start with the [service and export guide](/blog/remote-play-shuffle-tests). For a physical session, choose two lists below, assemble them, and write down what actually happened.
