# Invisible Woman: Build the Wall, Then Break the Table

The Fantastic Four precon is at its best when it feels like a team rather than a pile of four-color staples. With **Invisible Woman, Sue Storm** in the command zone, the deck has a clear identity: cast a noncreature spell, create the defensive infrastructure to survive, then turn that board into a safe and surprisingly wide attack.

The stock list already has a strong base. **Sol Ring, Arcane Signet, Farseek, Cultivate, Three Visits,** and **Chromatic Lantern** handle much of the fixing. **Invisible Force Field, Clever Concealment,** and the Wall-producing commander give the deck a defensive posture, while the Fantastic Four themselves provide alternate ways to build the deck later. The upgrade should not erase that identity. It should make Invisible Woman’s trigger happen more often and make every trigger matter more.

## The finding: this is defensive spellslinger tokens

Invisible Woman is not simply a four-color good-stuff commander, and she is not only a Wall commander. Her most reliable version sits at the intersection of three plans:

- **Spell velocity:** cast a cheap instant, sorcery, or other noncreature spell before combat.
- **Board development:** turn that spell into a Wall and let token engines create additional bodies.
- **Conversion:** use protection, high-toughness payoffs, token scaling, and attack pressure to turn a defensive board into a win.

That changes how cards should be evaluated. A cheap answer such as **An Offer You Can’t Refuse** is more valuable here than it would be in a normal midrange deck because it protects the engine, triggers Invisible Woman, and leaves mana available for the commander’s combat ability. A large creature that does none of those jobs may be powerful but still be a poor draw.

The first play-pattern rule is straightforward: **try to cast a noncreature spell before combat whenever it is safe to do so.** Do not burn the only spell in hand just to create a token if holding it protects the commander or stops a must-answer permanent. The goal is not to trigger Invisible Woman at any cost; it is to create a repeatable loop where your normal interaction also develops your board.

## Upgrade path one: make the commander reliable

The first wave should improve consistency before adding expensive token doublers. Start with cards that are useful when the battlefield is empty and still advance the commander plan when the board is established.

### First additions

- **Swords to Plowshares** gives the deck one-mana exile removal.
- **Counterspell** protects Invisible Woman, a token engine, or a decisive spell.
- **Beast Within** answers any permanent type.
- **Nature’s Lore** increases early fixing without adding another clunky mana rock.
- **An Offer You Can’t Refuse** is a cheap instant-speed trigger and protection spell.
- **Heroic Intervention** protects the board that the deck spends several turns building.
- **Swiftfoot Boots** gives Invisible Woman protection and haste.
- **Propaganda** buys time against creature-heavy tables.
- **Restorative Technique** provides inexpensive recovery for a destroyed engine.

This package is intentionally modest. It creates more hands that can ramp, cast the commander, and still hold up a spell for the next combat. It also keeps the deck from becoming dependent on drawing a specific token payoff.

## Upgrade path two: turn one spell into several bodies

Once the spell-and-protection floor is stable, add engines that multiply each noncreature spell.

**Shark Typhoon** is the standout upgrade. It can be cycled as an instant to trigger the commander, or it can remain on the battlefield and turn later noncreature spells into flying Sharks. That second mode is especially important because it gives the deck an evasive route to victory rather than asking Walls to do every job.

**Monastery Mentor, Third Path Iconoclast,** and **Young Pyromancer** are the budget-friendly spell-to-creature package. They make the deck’s ordinary interaction produce a board. **Storm-Kiln Artist** adds Treasures instead, which is valuable because the Fantastic Four’s activated abilities ask for colored mana and the deck often wants to cast more than one spell in a turn.

For card advantage, **Caretaker’s Talent, Idol of Oblivion,** and **Archmage Emeritus** keep the engine from running out of gas. Idol of Oblivion is particularly appropriate when the deck is producing tokens every turn; Archmage Emeritus is better when the build contains a heavier instant-and-sorcery package.

## Upgrade path three: make the Walls lethal

Invisible Woman’s Walls are not supposed to remain harmless forever. The cleanest conversion package is:

- **High Alert** — lets high-toughness creatures attack using toughness.
- **Assault Formation** — a second high-toughness payoff for a dedicated Wall version.
- **Axebane Guardian** — turns a cluster of Walls into a real mana engine.
- **Arcades, the Strategist** — a strong alternate commander and a useful 99-card Wall payoff.
- **Impact Tremors** — gives every new token an immediate damage role.

High Alert is the most important purchase in this package because it changes the purpose of the Wall tokens without requiring a large board. Impact Tremors is the best backup plan: even when the table makes combat difficult, repeated token creation still moves opponents toward a lethal range.

Do not add every Wall card at once. A token/spellslinger build wants enough creatures to defend itself, but it should not become so creature-heavy that it loses the cheap noncreature spells that turn Invisible Woman on.

## Upgrade path four: multiply the board and the mana

Token doublers are powerful only when the deck has a reliable token engine. Buy them after Shark Typhoon, a spell-to-creature payoff, and a card-draw engine are already working.

- **Parallel Lives** is the cleanest permanent token multiplier, but it is the largest early expense.
- **Adrix and Nev, Twincasters** provides a second multiplier and a creature body.
- **Cryptolith Rite** converts a wide token board into the colored mana needed for a major spell chain or multiple Fantastic Four activations.
- **Second Harvest** is the budget-friendly burst option when a permanent doubler is outside the budget.

The ideal sequence is not “cast a token doubler and hope.” It is “establish a token engine, cast the doubler, then use a cheap spell to trigger Invisible Woman and multiply the entire turn’s output.”

## Upgrade path five: protect the investment

A Wall/token deck is vulnerable to a single sweeper because its board is also its card advantage, mana, and win condition. Protection should be layered:

- Keep **Clever Concealment** from the precon.
- Add **Heroic Intervention** as the first broad protection upgrade.
- Use **Swiftfoot Boots** to protect the commander.
- Add cheap stack interaction such as **Counterspell** and **An Offer You Can’t Refuse**.
- Consider **Rootborn Defenses** when the deck has a wide token board.
- Treat **Fierce Guardianship** and **Deflecting Swat** as premium, cross-deck purchases rather than required upgrades.

The premium cards are excellent, but they are not where this deck gets its first major improvement. A $1–$3 spell that triggers Invisible Woman and answers a threat is often a better first purchase than a luxury free counterspell.

## A practical budget build

### Around $25: consistency and identity

Start with **Swords to Plowshares, Counterspell, Beast Within, Nature’s Lore, An Offer You Can’t Refuse, Tamiyo’s Safekeeping, Third Path Iconoclast, Young Pyromancer, High Alert, Impact Tremors, Idol of Oblivion,** and **Rootborn Defenses**.

This version interacts cheaply, creates more bodies, draws from its token plan, and turns high toughness into real combat pressure.

### Around $50: the deck starts to feel complete

Add **Shark Typhoon, Storm-Kiln Artist, Archmage Emeritus, Swiftfoot Boots, Propaganda, Caretaker’s Talent, Strionic Resonator,** and **Murmuring Mystic**.

Strionic Resonator is a particularly nice Invisible Woman upgrade because it provides a budget way to copy a triggered ability. It does not replace every function of Lithoform Engine, but it lets the deck reproduce an important commander trigger without paying premium prices.

### Around $100 and beyond: commitment upgrades

Add **Cryptolith Rite, Parallel Lives, Adrix and Nev, Twincasters, Improvisation Capstone, Echocasting Symposium,** and **Germination Practicum**. Only then consider **Doubling Season, Fierce Guardianship, Deflecting Swat, Rhystic Study,** or **Smothering Tithe**.

Those premium cards are better purchases when they can move between several decks in the collection. The full ManaBox export should be imported first so the tracker can identify cards already owned or sitting in other decks.

## What to cut from the stock list

The best cuts are not necessarily the weakest cards in isolation. They are the cards that do the least for Invisible Woman’s specific plan.

The first candidates to test are **Galactus, Devourer of Worlds, Silver Surfer, Galactus’s Herald, Mind’s Dilation, Genesis Ultimatum, Annie Joins Up, Mirage Mirror, Path of Discovery,** and **Taunt from the Rampart**. These cards are not unplayable, but they are slower, more situational, or less connected to creating and exploiting the Wall/token board.

Do not cut every sweeper. Keep enough reset effects to recover when an opposing deck develops faster. The upgrade goal is not to remove the precon’s ability to answer a board; it is to ensure that your own board is the one that benefits most from the next turn.

## Invisible Woman at the table

A good opening hand contains three lands or two lands plus ramp, one cheap noncreature spell, and either protection or a token engine. A hand full of six-mana creatures is not a keep simply because the deck has four colors.

Fix blue and green early when possible. Blue supports the cheap interaction and card flow; green supports the ramp and Wall-based mana plan. White and red become more important as the deck starts using its removal and Fantastic Four combat abilities.

Do not cast Invisible Woman automatically on turn four. If the table is not pressuring you, it can be better to establish an engine first or wait until you can cast her with protection. She is strongest when a spell is ready to trigger her and the resulting combat step is worth protecting.

## Final verdict

The best Invisible Woman upgrade is not the most expensive version. It is the version where every part of the deck does at least two jobs: interaction triggers the commander, tokens draw cards, Walls make mana, and protection also advances the board.

Start with cheap spells and reliable token engines. Add High Alert and Impact Tremors to create multiple ways to win. Then decide whether the deck wants permanent token multiplication or premium protection. If the deck consistently creates a board and survives the first sweeper, the expensive upgrades will feel like finishers rather than repairs.
