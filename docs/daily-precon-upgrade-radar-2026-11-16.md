## Today’s decision

Do not force a purchase today. MagicCon Atlanta has ended, Star Trek Commander has just entered the market, and the next major precon story is already forming around the reported Nauctis: The Sunken Realm decks. That combination creates a useful pause: enough supply is arriving to produce attractive reprint dips, but not enough information is available to know which new cards will be included in the next product wave.

The practical move is to reserve budget rather than spend it. Keep an eye on Academy Ruins, World at War, and Foundry Inspector. If they are already part of a shipping-efficient cart, they can become reasonable low-cost additions. If buying them requires a separate order, wait. A cheap card is not a cheap purchase when shipping costs more than the card.

The central question for the Precon Studio is no longer simply “What card is cheapest today?” It is “Which card can serve more than one deck after the release-weekend noise clears?”

## What changed

### Nauctis creates a future Merfolk bridge

The reported Nauctis: The Sunken Realm announcement points to four precons scheduled for February 5, 2027:

- **Merfolk Regiment** — Bant, with Captain Hanno Corolean; reported themes include go-wide Merfolk, tapping, and convoke.
- **Seal the Deal** — Boros, led by Gallorte; reported group-hug and Treasure themes.
- **Blood in the Water** — Izzet, led by Shredmouth; reported Sharks, Blood tokens, and discard.
- **Terrors of the Abyss** — Sultai, reportedly led by The Noble Serpent; reported self-mill, discard, and sea-monster reanimation.

Full decklists are not yet available in the supplied report. Treat these as product-watch themes, not confirmed card-buying instructions. The most actionable connection is **Merfolk Regiment** and the existing **Explorers of the Deep** deck. Bant adds white to the Simic Merfolk shell, which could increase interest in cards such as Merrow Reejerey, Deeproot Waters, and Merrow Commerce. It could also put pressure on the assumption that every older Merfolk staple will remain outside the next precon.

The correct collection move is to preserve the core cards you already own, not to buy speculative copies in bulk before the full list is known.

### Star Trek is still in the supply phase

Star Trek Commander released on November 13 with four decks: **We Are the Borg**, **Landing Party**, **Federation Fleet**, and **Klingon Fury**. Opened decks are adding copies of reprints to the market, which is pushing down prices on several attractive singles.

That supply is useful for players, but it creates a poor environment for chasing launch-weekend prices. A card can look like a bargain and still fall further as more boxes are opened. The best targets are cards with a role in several decks, a history of Commander utility, or a clear place in a build you already intend to play.

### Collector’s Edition remains a cosmetic lane

The reported Collector’s Edition precon prices remain far above standard decks. Unless the goal is sealed collecting or owning the Surge Foil treatments, the premium does not improve the gameplay of an upgrade package. For a $15 singles budget, standard nonfoil cards have a much stronger use case.

## Research Watch

The following cards are intentionally kept out of the confirmed mover stream. They have a plausible deck or market thesis, but the evidence is not strong enough to turn them into automatic buys.

### Academy Ruins — reprint dip / broad artifact utility

**Reported range:** approximately $1.41–$1.54 for the Star Trek reprint.  
**Status:** Watch.  
**Connected decks:** We Are the Borg, Keen Engineering, Necron Dynasties, and other artifact-heavy builds.

Academy Ruins is the strongest candidate in this report because it has utility beyond the deck that reprinted it. The release-weekend supply appears to have pushed it far below older Double Masters pricing. That is a meaningful opportunity if you actually play artifact decks, but it is not a reason to buy sealed We Are the Borg at a premium just to obtain a single land.

The failure case is straightforward: more copies continue entering the market, and the apparent floor becomes a temporary launch-weekend low. The better plan is to add one copy to an existing order or set a limit price below the current ask.

### Foundry Inspector — the shared curve smoother

**Reported range:** approximately $0.31–$0.49.  
**Status:** Watch / bundle only.  
**Connected decks:** We Are the Borg and Keen Engineering.

Foundry Inspector is exactly the kind of card the tracker should identify as a cross-deck bridge. It reduces the cost of a large artifact package, helps We Are the Borg reach its expensive commander and artifact-creature turns, and can move into Keen Engineering without becoming stranded in one list.

The card is cheap enough that the main risk is not price loss. It is inefficient acquisition. Do not pay a separate shipping charge for a sub-$1 card unless the seller cart already contains the rest of your planned upgrades.

### World at War — extra-combat pressure

**Reported price:** approximately $1.45 for the Star Trek reprint.  
**Status:** Watch / playable pickup.  
**Connected deck:** Klingon Fury.

World at War supports Klingon Fury’s aggressive identity by adding another way to convert a wide Warrior board into multiple combat steps. It is more compelling as a card for a deck you will actually play than as a short-term speculation target. Extra-combat cards can remain desirable, but the price is sensitive to reprints and the number of Commander decks that can use them.

The card becomes a reasonable buy under the stated budget when it is already in a consolidated order. Otherwise, give launch-weekend sellers more time to compete with one another.

### Open the Way — Landing Party land-name setup

**Reported range:** approximately $1.50–$2.50.  
**Status:** Developing.  
**Connected deck:** Landing Party.

Open the Way is a clean functional idea for Commander Spock. The deck wants differently named lands, and putting multiple nonbasic lands onto the battlefield can accelerate the point at which Spock’s land-name plan becomes meaningful. It may also help the deck recover after Spock is removed by giving the rest of the list a stronger landfall foundation.

This is not yet a verified market signal. It is a deck-fit candidate whose value depends on the exact Landing Party list, the final mana base, and whether players actually adopt the upgrade. Keep it in the Research Watch until those pieces are confirmed.

### Synthetic Destiny — Multiverse Reforged polymorph finisher

**Reported range:** approximately $0.50–$0.90.  
**Status:** Speculative.  
**Connected deck:** Multiverse Reforged.

Synthetic Destiny is an appealing fit for a deck that can create disposable tokens and wants to turn them into larger threats. Its instant speed also gives the deck a way to act at the end of an opponent’s turn rather than committing its entire plan at sorcery speed.

The risk is that a single decklist recommendation does not create durable demand. A card can be excellent in one Multiverse Reforged build while remaining bulk elsewhere. Test the card before buying multiples, and do not promote it into a confirmed mover without broader adoption or repeated price evidence.

### Merrow Commerce — early Merfolk positioning

**Reported range:** approximately $0.60–$0.95.  
**Status:** Speculative.  
**Connected decks:** Explorers of the Deep and a possible future Merfolk Regiment build.

Merrow Commerce is the most interesting forward-looking penny watch. A team-wide untap effect could support a deck that wants to tap Merfolk for convoke, trigger tap abilities, and still retain blockers or activated abilities afterward.

However, the future decklist is not known. Nauctis may include this card, a stronger replacement, or an entirely different engine. That makes buying ahead of the product reveal risky. Preserve copies already in the collection and wait for the January spoiler window before expanding the position.

### Fixing for five-color or broad-color builds

Arcane Sanctum, Exotic Orchard, and Command Tower remain inexpensive utility cards for decks that need better fixing. They are useful collection cards, but the report’s connection to Dance of the Elements should not be treated as a price catalyst by itself. Put them in a general fixing basket rather than a speculative purchase basket.

## Deck clinics

### We Are the Borg: lower the curve before adding more payoff

The stock deck’s most important improvement is not another expensive artifact threat. It is making the early turns more efficient. Foundry Inspector and Etherium Sculptor give the deck more opportunities to double-spell, keep mana open, and reach The Borg Queen without spending the entire turn on a four-mana rock.

Academy Ruins adds long-game recursion, but it should support the curve plan rather than replace it. The clean package is one cost reducer, one flexible rock cut, and one recursion land if the deck has enough artifacts worth recurring.

**Collection action:** reserve Foundry Inspector for both We Are the Borg and Keen Engineering only if you have enough physical copies. A card assigned to one deck should not automatically count as available to the other.

### Landing Party: increase differently named land density carefully

Commander Spock rewards differently named lands, but the deck should not replace every basic land with a fragile or expensive utility land. Growth Spiral and Tireless Tracker provide a bridge between land development and card advantage. Open the Way is a candidate for testing because it puts multiple lands into play, but the exact cuts should wait until the deck’s final mana base is reviewed.

**Collection action:** mark Open the Way as a developing upgrade, not a purchase-ready card. Add it to the basket only when the price and exact role are confirmed.

### Klingon Fury: make every attack matter

Klingon Fury wants to attack more than one opponent and turn those attacks into cards, tokens, and additional pressure. World at War supports the extra-combat identity, while Windcrag Siege can double attack-triggered abilities when its Mardu mode is chosen.

The package should not remove every thematic Warrior. Cutting too much of the deck’s creature density can make the extra-combat cards worse. Remove the narrowest card that does not reward attacking, create a token, or contribute to the deck’s sacrifice and damage plan.

**Collection action:** World at War is a playable under-$3 watch, while Windcrag Siege remains a bundle target until its exact printing and current supply are verified.

### Multiverse Reforged: test the polymorph lane before buying into it

Jace, Multiverse Architect and Proteus Staff establish the deck’s cheat-into-play identity. Synthetic Destiny is attractive because it can turn disposable tokens into a board of larger creatures at instant speed, but the plan is only good if the creature density and token production support it.

Treat Dack Fayden, Helping Hand as a bridge card rather than proof that every polymorph upgrade belongs in the deck. The best version of this clinic begins with a goldfish test and a small proxy package before committing cash.

## Old deck, new mechanic: Explorers of the Deep and Merfolk Regiment

The possible Bant Merfolk direction is the report’s most valuable collection insight. Explorers of the Deep already provides a Simic Merfolk foundation. A future Bant deck could add white-based interaction, tapping, and convoke without making those older Simic staples obsolete.

That does not mean buying every Merfolk card before spoilers. It means protecting the cards you already own and recording them as reserved strategic inventory. When Nauctis decklists arrive, compare the new list against Explorers of the Deep and identify exact overlap before purchasing anything.

The tracker should eventually show this as a bridge package:

- Cards already owned in Explorers of the Deep.
- Cards likely to transfer into Merfolk Regiment.
- Cards that remain in the Simic deck and require a second copy.
- New white cards needed to complete the Bant conversion.

This is a better use of the watchlist than a blind buy. The value is in knowing which cards you already control when the new deck arrives.

## Buy, wait, or leave alone

### Buy only inside an existing cart

- **Academy Ruins**, if you need it for an artifact deck and can obtain it without standalone shipping.
- **World at War**, if Klingon Fury is a deck you plan to play and the price remains near the reported sub-$2 range.
- **Foundry Inspector**, if it fills a real We Are the Borg or Keen Engineering curve gap.

### Wait for more evidence

- Star Trek singles above $3, including Heroic Intervention, Exploration, and premium Locutus of Borg treatments.
- Open the Way until the Landing Party mana base and exact deck role are verified.
- Merrow Commerce and other Merfolk speculation until Nauctis decklists and spoilers arrive.
- Synthetic Destiny until Multiverse Reforged adoption and pricing are independently confirmed.

### Leave alone for this budget plan

- Collector’s Edition Star Trek precons, unless the goal is cosmetic sealed collecting.
- Speculative Nauctis singles before the complete product lists are available.
- Any sub-$1 card that requires a separate shipping charge.
- Sealed product purchased solely to access one inexpensive reprint.
